# Close the Gap

A Windows Godot 4.7 game for two friends. The stalker has a knife and an aimed handgun; the sniper has a bolt-action rifle. First person, first to three round wins, alternating roles. The sniper wins a 150-second timeout. Knife and rifle eliminate in one hit. The handgun deals 40 damage to 100 health, has a six-round magazine, 12 spare rounds, a 0.4-second firing interval and 1.6-second reload. These are prototype balance choices awaiting human playtests. Smoke grenades are removed.

Pine Hollow adds a dense forest to the existing East Yard. Trees, ferns, grass and shrubs establish the setting; trunks, logs and rocks provide physical cover, while foliage conceals movement. Three connected trails and spawn clearings preserve traversable routes and some long sightlines. Geometry is deterministic across peers. Shared meshes and MultiMesh batches keep foliage construction bounded. Both bots respect shrub concealment. The stalker bot crouches through covered flanks, circles behind the perch and attempts only rear knife attacks; its handgun remains unused. Humans retain the handgun option. The sniper has directional sight and no rear proximity vision. Pine Hollow now rises 18 metres to a ridge with covered outer approaches. East Yard retains the container routes and walkable sniper platform and adds screened side ramps and a rear bridge. The shared player simulation confines snipers to their ridge or deck. Both roles can move on the high ground; only the stalker can leave it.

Host and practice menus select the map. The host's selected map is included in round configuration; the guest's previous local choice cannot override it. The stalker switches with 1/2, aims the handgun with RMB and reloads with R. Knife remains available after ammunition is exhausted. The HUD shows the current map, weapon, magazine/reserve, reload state and own health. Sniper scoping uses an optical overlay; handgun aiming keeps the iron sights visible.

The host validates inputs, simulates movement, owns ammunition and health, resolves attacks and determines results. The guest predicts its movement and receives host state. Reliable messages carry round transitions and combat effects; ordered unreliable messages carry inputs and snapshots. Internet friend invites use the free open-source Noray test service, with direct connection attempts and relay fallback. The game establishes certificate-pinned DTLS and validates a private expiring invite before allowing gameplay RPCs. LAN/direct-IP remains a separate unencrypted test path. Connection ownership is isolated in scripts/network/friend_connection.gd so Steam can replace rendezvous and transport later without changing combat or round simulation. See networking-direction.md for details and verified limits.

The visual identity remains blue steel, chalk and ochre in the interface. The forest adds muted pine, moss, earth and stone colors. Existing Ubuntu Condensed display type and Ubuntu body type remain. Visual verification uses an authenticated private virtual display; no window is brought onto the user’s desktop. Windows execution, audio and subjective game feel still need human testing.

Finish condition for the level/combat update: both maps selectable, deterministic and connected forest routes with dense foliage, handgun aiming/damage/reload/limits functional in practice and over two real local peers, existing knife/rifle and round behavior preserved, and a refreshed Windows export and source ZIP. The implemented friend connection flow and its validation limits are recorded separately in networking-direction.md.

The app supports local bot duels with both POVs side by side. The bundled recorded session is now its default launch mode. Each pane shows the bot’s weapon, health, behavior and score; the stalker pane also reports whether it has been spotted. Pause, restart, level selection and Menu remain accessible. Matches restart automatically. Per-bot controllers own perception and remembered state, while the match continues to own authoritative movement, damage and results. Research and tuning evidence are in bot-tuning.md.

The forest environment now adds textured bark and leaf-covered soil, open pine boughs, individual broad leaves, and layers of grasses, ferns, brambles, roots, stumps, saplings, stones, mushrooms and natural debris. Detail placement has a separate fixed seed and keeps tactical routes clear. Reusable plant meshes, local batches, color variation and position-varied breeze motion are informed by the published OpenGOAL Jak decompilation; references and asset provenance are in forest-art.md.

Tall grass patches add concealment along the forest understory. Deer, foxes and rabbits walk and graze as ambient wildlife. Tree birds are a gameplay cue: uncrouched noisy movement or gunfire can startle nearby flocks, producing visible flight and spatial calls. The host owns disturbance events; only rough tree locations reach the sniper’s perception. Quiet crouched movement avoids movement-triggered alarms. See forest-art.md for exact rules and limits.

The bundled ten-minute headless recording is now the default viewing mode. Replay applies recorded states and events to the shared two-POV spectator, with a seek bar, speed controls, pause and restart. AI and gameplay physics are disabled during replay. Live bots remain available from the controls and menu. The recording uses 60 Hz simulation, 20 Hz state samples and event timing at simulation ticks; recorded-session.md describes fidelity and limits.
