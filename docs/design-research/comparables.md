# Close the Gap: comparable-game research

## Decision frame

Close the Gap is a small-scope, friend-versus-friend 1v1: a stalker must cross a physical forest and reach a rear knife kill, while a mobile high-ground sniper reads direct sight and approximate sound. The current 150-second timeout and one-hit rifle create a real risk of passive sniper play. The comparisons below are evidence about shipped designs, not proof that their solutions transfer to this game. Human playtests remain the balance authority; bot outcomes are not human evidence.

## Transferable hypotheses (proposals, not sourced facts)

1. **Pressure should be local and physical.** A sniper timeout should not end by revealing the stalker globally. Instead, test a rule that makes prolonged nest occupation produce a visible/ audible environmental consequence near that nest, or makes a previously used firing angle less safe. Success criterion: in human rounds, sniper relocation increases without making the stalker’s position explicit.
2. **A shot should spend information.** After a missed or nonlethal handgun shot, the stalker has created a rough sound clue but should not receive an exact sniper location. After a sniper shot, the sniper should have to choose between holding a known angle and moving along a connected trail. Success criterion: both players can explain what they learned and what they gave away.
3. **Give the stalker a short, testable approach objective.** Rather than adding inventory or abilities, place one or two physical interaction opportunities on routes (for example, a crossing that can be prepared quietly or a wildlife perch that can be disturbed deliberately). The interaction must have a readable cost and a counter-read by the sniper. Success criterion: stalkers choose different approaches and snipers respond to cues rather than waiting for timeout.
4. **Use paired role swaps as the engagement loop.** SpyParty’s manual recommends alternating roles; that recommendation is not measured evidence of a learning effect. As a design hypothesis, score complete role pairs (each player gets one stalker and one sniper turn) before declaring a match winner, and expose round-end evidence (last known cue, shot direction, time remaining). The current first-to-three structure can end after an odd number of rounds and give one player an extra sniper turn. Test a short review with the existing replay before building an elaborate kill-cam.
5. **Tune uncertainty before adding content.** Start with one forest, the existing seven nests and current fauna cues. Test cue radius, persistence/fading and route visibility in matched human rounds. Add a new mechanic only if players can state a concrete decision it enables.

## Scope and pitfalls

- **Camping countermeasures can become a wallhack.** Any anti-passivity cue should be approximate, delayed and spatially local; never reveal an exact position, role roster or spawn. Keep it compatible with direct sight and sound-only bot perception.
- **High-ground fairness is not fixed by symmetry.** The sniper’s rifle is one-hit and unlimited; giving the stalker more weapons or a scope-like reveal would erase the intended asymmetry. Prefer route/attention trade-offs, shot commitment and environmental tells.
- **Do not mistake comparable scale for a requirement.** Sniper Elite’s campaign invasion relies on authored missions, NPCs and traversal breadth; Deathloop relies on a large systemic sandbox and matchmaking; SpyParty relies on a dense authored tell language. Borrow the information pattern, not the production surface area.
- **Bots cannot validate engagement.** Existing bot data can check physical-perception and route invariants, but only human friend pairs can tell whether uncertainty feels fair, whether timeout pressure is motivating, and whether role swaps sustain a session.
- **Measure the intended behavior.** In a small playtest, record: time spent stationary at one nest, number of relocations after shots, stalker route changes after cues, timeout frequency, and each player’s predicted last-known location before the round ends. Ask whether a loss was attributed to a readable decision or an unknowable hit.

## Pressure-option critique (design hypothesis)

This is a proposal for a human test plan, not a sourced claim. The brief’s core fantasy is still “find the unseen rear approach and kill the opponent”; pressure should make waiting less dominant without replacing that with an unrelated task game.

| Option | Fit with the fantasy | Main risk | Recommendation / counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Kill-only; timeout is a draw; paired role swaps** | Strongest fit. Every decisive result is still a kill, and a sniper cannot convert patience into a win. | A draw can feel empty, especially if both players learn that hiding is rational. | Use as the control condition. Preserve the 150-second cap, swap roles in paired rounds, and measure draw rate plus stationary time. The stalker’s counterplay is route pressure and uncertainty; the sniper’s is choosing when a shot is worth exposing the nest. If draws dominate, do not immediately add chores—test a smaller cap or clearer environmental tells first. |
| **B. Relay variant: 2 of 3 stations offline starts a repair deadline** | Potentially compatible if stations create a trail crossing or expose a nest. It gives the stalker a reason to move and the defender a reason to hunt. | It alters the pure kill contract, adds level-authored work, and can become a chore loop where the defender patrols repairs or the attacker speed-runs stations. | Prototype separately as an optional mode. Each station can be sabotaged once; restored stations stay restored. A missed repair deadline gives an explicit infiltration win; lethal attacks end the round immediately. Use short interactions and local world/audio evidence. Interruptions require an observable action, damage or loss of physical access; no invisible proximity contests. The [roadmap](../design-roadmap.md) specifies complete timing and round-cap rules. |
| **C. Kill-only; radio check-ins, forced relocations, or automatic reveal** | Weak fit. It dictates behavior from outside the physical sight/sound fiction and risks turning the sniper into a timer-managed target. | Forced movement and reveal punish a legitimate sniper read, can feel like GPS/wallhack logic, and may make the stalker wait for the system instead of learning the terrain. | Reject for now. If a local anti-camp rule is needed, prefer a delayed, approximate physical consequence (disturbed wildlife, a changing sightline, or a nest becoming less useful) that both roles can understand and counterplay. Avoid exact location reveals or compulsory “chores.” |

**Recommendation:** run A first as the pure-kill, paired-role control. Then test B only as a separately labeled optional mode if A shows unacceptable draw/stall rates in human pairs; the lead brief must choose and measure its exact deadline win rule. Compare not only wins, but whether players report pursuing the rear-knife plan, whether defenders make active searches, and whether the added objective is described as an opportunity or a chore. Do not merge B into the core rules until it demonstrably creates more kill attempts without making sabotage the dominant win condition.
